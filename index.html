Station

Solo project · procedural multiplayer extraction horror · in development

Featured · main projectA procedurally generated station, 250+ hand-made assets, and a multiplayer horror loop built to make you feel alone. +
Procedural GenerationMultiplayer250+ Models UV'd & TexturedLightingPost ProcessUI / UXUnity / C#

The gameA multiplayer extraction horror game built on procedural generation. The goal is dread through isolation: even with friends in the same session, the station should make you feel alone.

The artI modeled, UV-unwrapped, and textured more than 250 assets by hand, then lit and post-processed every space so the mood lands the moment a player steps in. Hero pieces and background clutter get the same care, because that is what makes a corridor feel lived in.

The techProcedural level generation and multiplayer horror are two areas I think are underexplored, so I am pushing on both at once. Alien: Isolation, SOMA, and Portal 2 set the bar I am building toward.

My roleEverything: design, programming, 3D modeling, UV and texturing, lighting, post-processing, and UI. This is my main portfolio piece and it is in active development.

Shift

Solo project · gravity-switching first-person arena shooter

 +
Unreal / BlueprintsLightingLevel Design

The gameA fast, arcade-style movement shooter where stepping onto a plane flips your gravity. Built for 3v3 play across several modes, with levels designed so players keep finding creative routes through a fight.

My roleI designed the gravity mechanic, using volume triggers to reorient players onto new surfaces, and built a weapon and ability system players choose at spawn. I also designed and lit multiple levels so every angle reads cinematically, whichever way up happens to be.

What it taught meShift was my first project to outgrow its scope. Cutting features, locking down a core mechanic, and planning around constraints are habits I now bring to day one of every project.

About

I have been taking games apart to see how they work since I was young. Tutorials and solo experiments got me started, and Shift, a project far out of scope, taught me what matters most: a clear core mechanic, honest scoping, and finishing what you start. I bring that to every project now.

I am a Digital Media student at the University of Central Florida, graduating Spring 2027. Technical art is where I found my place: it sits between modeling and programming, and it lets me do the most for a team. I am happiest building the system, modeling the assets, and lighting the scene so it all works together.

Right now I am working on team projects for Workshop and Casual Game Production alongside Station, my own game. I write modular, optimized, well-commented code so teammates can pick it up without me, and I am looking for a team that cares about craft as much as I do.
